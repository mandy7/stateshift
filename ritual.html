Three minutes
nobody is using.

They are already in your day, somewhere between the last thing and the next thing. Here is what they are for, and exactly how the cup works.

We made it slow on purpose

The first version of StateShift was instant. Powder, hot water, done in twenty seconds. It worked. It was also completely pointless.

Because the thing I actually needed, a door between one part of the day and the next, does not happen in twenty seconds. It happens in the three minutes after the water goes in, when nothing at all is being asked of you.

The steep isn't you waiting for the coffee.
It's the coffee waiting for you.

So the slowness is designed in. Three minutes is long enough to be a pause and short enough that you will actually take it. Nothing to grind, nothing to weigh, nothing to wash up. The cup just gives your hands something to hold while you arrive.

Why two sachets, and not one

This is the question we get most, usually with a slightly suspicious look. Two sachets seems like one too many.

But what should dissolve and what should steep are not the same thing. Creamer, jaggery and cocoa want to melt straight into water. Coffee grounds and whole aromatics want the opposite. They need to be held apart from the cup, steeped, then lifted out and thrown away.

We tried it in one sachet. It was gritty, it was muddy, and the aroma never lifted off the surface. Keeping them apart until the water brings them together is the difference between a clean cup and a cup you politely finish.

It looks like extra packaging. It's actually the whole product.

Sachet A · the filter

Everything that steeps

A pour-over filter pouch that hangs on the rim of your cup. Nothing gets into the coffee except flavour.

  • Freshly ground coffee single estate, ground for steeping, never in advance
  • Aromatics cardamom, mace, orange peel, by blend
Sachet B · the cup mix

Everything that dissolves

Tipped into the empty cup before the water. Body and sweetness, weighed to the tenth of a gram.

  • Creamer body that survives the pour
  • Palm jaggery warmth, not dessert
  • Cocoa where the blend calls for it

Six small steps

You need a cup and hot water. That's the entire equipment list.

  1. Empty the cup mixTip Sachet B into your empty cup. It should look like nothing much. It isn't.
  2. Hang the filterOpen Sachet A and hook the drip bag over the rim.
  3. Pour slowlyAbout 150ml of hot water, 90 to 95°C. Take 45 to 60 seconds over it. Not a step to rush. It is the first half of the pause.
  4. Steep and waitTwo to three minutes. Don't stir, don't poke it, don't check your phone. This is the part that matters.
  5. Lift the filter outStraight up, no squeezing. Squeezing is where bitterness comes from. Discard.
  6. Stir, then drinkTwenty to thirty seconds, until it's one cup and not two layers. Then take a breath before the first sip.

"Is it tea? Is it coffee?"

Everyone asks. It hangs like a tea bag because it is a pour-over filter. Inside is freshly ground single-estate coffee, not leaves. (One blend does borrow a little tea. We'll tell you which.) Nobody asks twice.

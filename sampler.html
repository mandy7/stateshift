Why order now and not later

One day this will be a thing on a shelf. Right now it is a bench you can pull a chair up to.

These cups are weighed on a kitchen scale, by one person, on the morning they go out. The batch is small enough to sign, and it gets signed.

Some of these blends will change after you drink them, partly because of what you tell me. Lamhe is still on the bench. Sehar is still hunting a bergamot I cannot buy in this country. Whatever you say goes into the same notebook as every breakthrough on the Lab page.

If you want the finished article, wait for the shelf. If you want your fingerprints on it, this is the only window that exists.

What if I don't like it?

Tell me and I will refund you. No form, no argument. I would rather know. Almost everything on the Lab page came from someone saying a cup was plain, or once, memorably, half-cooked.

Is it tea or coffee?

Coffee. It hangs on your cup like a tea bag because it is a pour-over filter, but inside is freshly ground single-estate coffee. Sehar borrows a little Earl Grey for its citrus, and that is the only tea in the box.

Do I need a machine, a grinder, anything?

A cup and hot water. That is the entire equipment list. Everything that needs grinding has already been ground, that morning, by hand.

How long does the coffee keep?

Drink it within a month. The sachets are sealed, but aromatics fade faster than coffee does, and the aroma is most of the point. Do not save these for a special occasion. The occasion is Tuesday.

Before you go

Every day was the same.
I just hadn't noticed.

StateShift did not begin with coffee. It began with paternity leave, an infant daughter, and the smell of wet earth in April.

The month I stopped noticing

Somewhere in there I stopped being able to tell one week from the next. Groundhog Day, except Bill Murray at least knew he was stuck.

Then my daughter was born and I stepped away from work. Suddenly the day had seams in it. One hour I was a father, holding an infant who has no opinion about my inbox. The next I was at a laptop trying to think in straight lines. Then a son, then a husband, then briefly just a person who wanted twenty minutes that belonged to nobody.

Nobody gives you a moment between them. You are expected to simply switch, so you don't. You carry the last hour into the next one and end up half-present in both.

What I needed wasn't more energy. It was a door between the rooms.

It started with rain

That April it rained properly for the first time in months. I stepped outside and the smell of wet earth put me, without asking, in a courtyard I had not stood in for twenty-five years.

Nothing moves you between states faster than smell. Not caffeine, not a to-do list. Aroma arrives before thought does.

So I stopped reading about coffee and started reading about perfume. I wanted to know whether a cup could be composed the same way, with a beginning, a middle and an after.

But an aroma you can't drink is just a candle. The coffee had to be good, and good coffee in India usually means a café, a queue and four hundred rupees, or a shelf of equipment you will use twice. So the format became part of the design. Two sachets, hot water, three minutes.

A kitchen counter and a great deal of bad coffee

All of it happens at a kitchen counter in North India: a hand grinder, a 0.1-gram scale, and a notebook that no longer closes properly.

Mostly it has been failure. Cardamom that disappeared into milk. Cardamom that turned the cup into chai. Spice that tasted bright on Monday and bitter by Friday, because volatile oils do not care about your schedule. Sachets that leaked. Cups my wife described, with devastating economy, as plain. Once, memorably, as half-cooked.

The rules came out of the failures, not the plan. Grind fresh. Nothing sits. Nothing gets rescued with sugar. If an ingredient cannot earn its place at a tenth of a gram, it does not get in.

The notebook · May 25 "aroma survived the milk. she found the cardamom without being asked. breakthrough."

Then one May morning she named the cardamom from across the kitchen, unprompted. That was the first cup that worked. Everything since, failures especially, is in the Lab.

What we believe

Coffee is the medium, not the product

We are not selling you a better bean. We are selling you back three minutes you already own.

Slow first, then fast

Every cup here takes three minutes you cannot skip. That is not an inconvenience we failed to design out. It is the part we designed in.

A cup that fits everywhere fits nowhere

Each blend is made for one part of the day, and honest about which. If it works equally well at 7am and 10pm, it wasn't designed. It was averaged.

Nothing gets rescued with sugar

If a cup only works because it is sweet, the cup does not work. Palm jaggery is there to round an edge, never to cover one. Ingredients that cannot earn a tenth of a gram do not get in.

I would rather make something a few hundred people love
than something ten thousand people quite like.
Mandhir

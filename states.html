Nothing here was guessed.
All of it was brewed.

Fifty-one sittings, logged with the verdict intact. Most taught us what not to make. Six of them changed the coffee, and those six are below.

The breakthrough

She named the cardamom from across the kitchen.

Nobody asked her to. She was doing something else. For months the aroma had been vanishing into the milk, and then one morning it did not, and she said the word out loud from six feet away. That cup is Rukh, and nothing about it has changed since.

Rukh · sitting 25
The thing nobody expects

The bitterness was never the coffee.

We chased it through grind size, dose, water temperature and steep time for weeks. It was the cardamom. Ground at home it oxidises within days and turns bitter, and it takes the whole cup down with it. Sealed commercial powder, fresh batch, fixed in one sitting. Aromatics are more fragile than the beans they sit next to.

Rukh · Kisse
The rule that came out of a mistake

120ml, or it is a different drink.

Ten millilitres either side and Rukh stops being Rukh. Too little and the cardamom shouts; too much and the coffee thins out and the whole thing goes flat. We only found the edge by crossing it, twice, on consecutive mornings.

Rukh · sittings 31 and 32
The one that took eleven tries

The coffee disappeared into the milk.

Cup after cup of Baarbara Estate came out tasting like cardamom milk with a rumour of coffee in it. Beautiful beans, wrong job. It took eleven sittings to accept that the bean was the problem and not the dose.

Rukh · sittings 3 to 14
The accident worth keeping

Mace, at a dose you could mistake for nothing.

A tenth of a gram of mace does something to Kisse that nobody has been able to name. Testers reach for whiskey, for smoke, for a spice they cannot place. Double it and the effect vanishes entirely, which is the strangest part.

Kisse
What is open right now

Hazelnut, and a citrus we are still chasing.

Lamhe needs a signature aroma the way Rukh has cardamom, and roasted hazelnut is the current candidate. Sehar is still being tuned: Earl Grey works as a citrus lift up to a point, and past about a tenth of a gram it stops being citrus and starts being black tea. Both are unfinished, and both are on this page before they are solved.
